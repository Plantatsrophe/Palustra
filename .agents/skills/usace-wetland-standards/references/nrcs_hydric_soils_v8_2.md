# NRCS Field Indicators of Hydric Soils (Version 8.2)

This document provides the regulatory definitions, Munsell matrix criteria, redoximorphic threshold requirements, and regional application rules for hydric soils under **NRCS Field Indicators of Hydric Soils in the United States (Version 8.2)**, as adopted in the USACE Eastern Mountains and Piedmont (EMP) and Atlantic and Gulf Coastal Plain (AGCP) Regional Supplements.

---

## 1. Technical Principles of Hydric Soils

Hydric soils form under conditions of saturation, flooding, or ponding long enough during the growing season to develop anaerobic conditions in the upper part.

### Key Biogeochemical Processes
1. **Soil Saturation**: Water fills soil pore spaces, cutting off atmospheric oxygen diffusion.
2. **Microbial Respiration**: Aerobic microorganisms consume dissolved oxygen within 24 to 72 hours.
3. **Reduction of Iron and Manganese**: Anaerobic microbes reduce insoluble ferric iron ($\text{Fe}^{3+}$) to soluble ferrous iron ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$), and insoluble manganese ($\text{Mn}^{4+}$) to soluble manganese ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$).
4. **Segregation & Depletion**: Soluble $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ is either mobilized and stripped (leaving gray/depleted matrices) or transported to aerobic microsites where it oxidizes to form bright orange/red/brown **redox concentrations** (masses, pore linings, nodules).

## 2. Munsell Color Specifications & Depleted Matrix Definitions

All matrix and redox colors must be determined using standard **Munsell Soil Color Charts** under natural daylight conditions on fresh, moist soil samples.

### Technical Definition of a Depleted Matrix
A **Depleted Matrix** is a layer in which the iron and manganese have been largely removed, or in which iron has been reduced to produce low chroma/high value colors. It must satisfy one of the following exact criteria:

| Category | Matrix Value | Matrix Chroma | Required Redox Concentrations ($\ge 2\text{ mm}$, distinct or prominent) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **1** | $\ge 5$ | $\le 1$ | None required (with or without redox concentrations) |
| **2** | $\ge 6$ | $\le 2$ | None required (with or without redox concentrations) |
| **3** | $4$ or $5$ | $2$ | $\ge 2\%$ distinct or prominent redox concentrations |
| **4** | $4$ | $1$ | $\ge 2\%$ distinct or prominent redox concentrations |

### Technical Definition of a Gleyed Matrix
A **Gleyed Matrix** exhibits mineral colors resulting from severe, prolonged anaerobiosis on the Munsell **Gley 1** or **Gley 2** pages:
* **Value Requirement**: Value must be $\ge 4.0$.
* **Qualifying Hues**:
  * *Gley 1 Chart*: 10Y, 5GY, 10GY, 5G, 10G, 5BG, 10BG, 5B, 10B, 5PB, or neutral (N).
  * *Gley 2 Chart*: 5PB, 10PB, 5P, 10P, 5RP.

| **A2** | **Histic Epipedon** | Organic soil material $\ge 20\text{ cm}$ ($8\text{ in.}$) thick starting within $10\text{ cm}$ ($4\text{ in.}$) of the surface. | Wet depressions, swales, seepage wetlands. |

| **A12** | **Thick Dark Surface** | A depleted or gleyed matrix starting within $30\text{ cm}$ beneath a thick dark mineral surface layer ($\ge 30\text{ cm}$ thick with Value $\le 3.5$, Chroma $\le 1$). | Organic-rich coastal wetland depressions. |

| **S7** | **Dark Surface** | Layer of sand $\ge 10\text{ cm}$ thick with Value $\le 3$, Chroma $\le 1$ underlain by Chroma $\le 2$. | Poorly drained sandy soils. |
| **S9** | **Thin Dark Surface** | Layer of sand $\ge 5\text{ cm}$ thick with Value $\le 3$, Chroma $\le 1$. | AGCP coastal barrier swales. |

### C. Loamy and Clayey Soil Indicators

| Code | Indicator Name | Criteria and Horizon Standards | Regional Specifics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **F2** | **Loamy Gleyed Matrix** | Layer $\ge 15\text{ cm}$ thick starting within $30\text{ cm}$ with a gleyed matrix. | Wet floodplains, swamp basins. |
| **F3** | **Depleted Matrix** | Depleted matrix layer $\ge 5\text{ cm}$ thick starting within $10\text{ cm}$, OR $\ge 15\text{ cm}$ thick starting within $25\text{ cm}$ of the surface. | The single most ubiquitous hydric soil indicator in EMP & AGCP. |
| **F6** | **Redox Dark Surface** | Layer $\ge 10\text{ cm}$ thick starting within $20\text{ cm}$ with dark matrix (Value $\le 3$, Chroma $\le 1$) and $\ge 2\%$ distinct or prominent redox concentrations. | Mucky loams, wet prairies. |

| **F12** | **Iron-Manganese Masses** | Layer $\ge 10\text{ cm}$ thick with $\ge 2\%$ soft iron-manganese masses within $30\text{ cm}$. | Long-term perched water tables. |

## 4. Regional Specific Hydric Soil Indicators (EMP vs AGCP)

### Indicator F19 - Piedmont Floodplain Soils (EMP ONLY)
* **Geographic Restriction**: Restricted strictly to Land Resource Region (LRR) P and Major Land Resource Area (MLRA) 148 in LRR N (Piedmont region).
* **Criteria**: On active floodplains, a layer at least $15\text{ cm}$ ($6\text{ in.}$) thick starting within $25\text{ cm}$ ($10\text{ in.}$) of the mineral soil surface with:
  * Matrix Value $\le 4$ and Chroma $\le 2$, AND
  * $\ge 2\%$ distinct or prominent redox concentrations occurring as pore linings or soft masses.
* **Regulatory Context**: Accommodates recent mineral sediment deposition that masks typical depleted matrix formation in dynamic Piedmont alluvial corridors.

### Indicator F20 - Anomalous Bright Loamy Soils (AGCP ONLY)
* **Geographic Restriction**: Restricted strictly to MLRAs 133A, 137, 149A, 153A, and 153B in LRRs T and U.
* **Criteria**: A layer at least $10\text{ cm}$ ($4\text{ in.}$) thick starting within $30\text{ cm}$ ($12\text{ in.}$) of the surface with:
  * Matrix Value $\ge 5$ and Chroma 3 or 4, AND
  * $\ge 10\%$ distinct or prominent redox concentrations.
* **Regulatory Context**: Designed for bright loamy coastal sediments containing high levels of goethite, where high chroma persists despite prolonged anaerobic saturation.
